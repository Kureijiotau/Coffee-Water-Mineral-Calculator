---
name: Recipe concentrate stock mass
description: Recipe equivalents and total stock weight are separate; historical mL stocks must not become grams.
---

For new recipe concentrates, ×1 contains the base recipe salt amounts for one liter of finished water, regardless of target stock weight. ×N contains N times those amounts. Total stock weight changes mg per stock gram and the gram dose, not the recipe's salt totals. Historical volume-based stocks retain their original units until explicitly rebuilt.

An editable stock dose in g/L is a recipe-preserving control: derive stock strength from finished stock grams divided by the selected g/L dose. If stock weight changes, retain the g/L setting and recalculate strength unless a pinned per-drop target is active. Editing the g/L dose clears that conflicting pin. For an unsafe implied stock strength, recommend a larger g/L dose at the existing conservative safe-strength recommendation.

**Why:** The owner requires w/w preparation and dosing everywhere, while preserving the established one-liter recipe-equivalent meaning of ×N; changing both denominators can cause a large dosing error. The user also wants to choose how many grams of stock to use while still reaching the recipe target.

**How to apply:** Compute physical salt from canonical targets, hydration, and actual recipe equivalents. Dose total stock grams divided by actual equivalents per liter. Keep any editable grams-per-liter dose as the strength source unless a drop target is pinned; use the existing modeled safety ceiling and never imply it is a laboratory guarantee. Use counted finished-stock mass for measured drops; disclose water-based estimates. A separate-salt mass anchor scales preparation size and actual equivalents together, retaining potency and the reference control.
