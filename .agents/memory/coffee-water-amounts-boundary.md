description: Quantitative research boundary for ion unpleasantness thresholds, ratios, and total mineral balance

Treat ion taste thresholds and “ideal” ratios as context-dependent guidance, not universal chemistry rules. In the R. Asami guide, tie each tasting prompt to the actual ion changes between recipes and ask users to record what they notice (for example, finish or juiciness) without implying a fixed effect.

**Why:** Brew method, coffee, pH, alkalinity, hardness, total mineral load, and sensory context interact; published brand recipes are practical conventions rather than universal optima, and coffee-specific unpleasantness data is sparse. The user wants to connect specific ion shifts, such as sulfate versus chloride, with their own cup observations.

**How to apply:** Keep ranges labeled by evidence type and matrix (water versus beverage). Frame the user's light-roast tasting data as a structured starting sandbox, not universal palate guidance. In prompts, name which ions or hardness contributions changed (including simultaneous GH/KH changes), then ask about observed finish, juiciness, acidity, or body for that coffee and brew. Separate experimentally supported relationships from heuristics, especially Cl:SO4 and simple total-ppm targets. Do not change solver ceilings or targets without separate validation.
