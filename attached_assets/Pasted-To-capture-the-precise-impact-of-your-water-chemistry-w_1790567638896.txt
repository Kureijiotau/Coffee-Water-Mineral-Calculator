To capture the precise impact of your water chemistry without typing out paragraphs, you need to shift the UI from purely scoring the water to profiling it.Right now, your 0–10 scales are great for rating how much you liked the cup, but a "10" for Mouthfeel doesn't tell you if the water made the coffee perfectly tea-like or perfectly syrupy.   Here are the structural changes and specific descriptors to evolve this interface.1. Structural Change: Bipolar Spectrum SlidersWater chemistry is a push-and-pull system, much like an audio EQ. Adding a section of bipolar sliders (e.g., -5 to +5, or simply dragging a point between two anchors) allows you to log the exact direction the water pushed the coffee, completely eliminating the need to write notes.Acidity Focus: Sharp / Articulate <--------> Mellow / Rounded (Tracks the Mg vs. Ca shift)Body / Weight: Tea-like / Weightless <--------> Heavy / CoatingStructure: Isolated / Monotone <--------> Complex / Layered (Perfect for tracking what happened when you dropped calcium in V41 vs V42)Finish: Fast / Fleeting <--------> Lingering / LongBy glancing at these four sliders, you will instantly know the physical shape of the extraction without reading a single word. You can keep the 0–10 "Water contribution" section purely as an objective quality score (e.g., "It was very tea-like, and I rate that a 9/10 for this bean").   2. Descriptors to Add to "Cup effect"Your current toggles (Crisp, Rounded, Thin, Full, etc.) are a solid foundation. Expanding them with the specific phenomena that water chemistry directly triggers will make the data much more actionable.   Structure & Tactile:Hollow: Crucial for logging pure-magnesium waters where the mid-palate drops out.Juicy: When acidity and body integrate well without becoming syrupy.Syrupy: For high-calcium, high-buffer extractions.Acidity & Flavor:Sparkling: For high-clarity, unmuted acids (low KH).Tart: When the malic/citric acids push slightly too hard.Jammy: When acids melt into the sugars (usually Ca dominant).Extremes & Defects:Tannic / Astringent: Essential for logging when the buffer (KH) is too low to neutralize wild fermentation notes.Muddy: When the buffer is too high or the ratio shifts too far, killing clarity.Flat: When acidity is overly neutralized.3. UI Grouping SuggestionInstead of lumping all descriptors under a single "Cup effect" accordion, split them into three distinct horizontal toggle groups: Acidity/Sweetness, Body/Tactile, and Finish/Defects. This reduces cognitive load, letting you quickly tap "Sparkling" in row one, "Hollow" in row two, and "Clean" in row three, creating a complete tasting profile in three clicks.
